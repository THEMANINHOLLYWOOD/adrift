    Where the animal comes from

    On October 26, 1873, off Portugal Cove in Conception Bay, Newfoundland, two fishermen rowed up to something floating that they took for a large sail or the debris of a wreck. One of them struck it. It reared a beak they described as “as big as a six-gallon keg”, struck the boat, and wound two arms around it. One of the men cut the arms off with a small axe, and the animal moved off in a cloud of ink.

    Nineteen feet of one arm reached the Rev. Moses Harvey in St. John’s. He called it “one of the rarest curiosities in the whole animal kingdom”. With a whole animal netted at Logy Bay a few weeks later, it gave zoologists proof that the kraken of sailors’ stories was real: the giant squid.

    In later tellings the axe is swung by a twelve-year-old, Tom Piccot. The letters written at the time mention only the two men.

    Giant squid eyes are real as well: up to eleven inches across, among the largest of any animal, made for catching faint light in deep water.

    The man, his son, the water world and the two robots are invented. The animal is not.

    Sources: the 1873–74 letters of Alexander Murray and Moses Harvey, quoted at Skulls in the Stars; Dictionary of Canadian Biography; Product of Newfoundland; The Jakarta Post; Wikipedia, “Giant squid”.
